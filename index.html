AEGIS OT Product Design Rationale & Architectural Whitepaper

1. Information Prioritization (The 5–10 Second Rule)

In industrial operational environments, critical physical downtime can incur millions of dollars per hour or create catastrophic safety hazards. We prioritize Crown Jewel Exposure and Cross-Zone Traversal at the top of the information hierarchy. The executive hero row answers immediately: Is the plant safe? (Posture Score 82/100, 4 critical findings, 3 exposed crown jewels, 1 confirmed active path).

2. Supporting Leadership vs. Analyst Workflows

  • CISO / Leadership: Receives high-level posture metrics, downtime impact estimates, monitoring coverage, and trend analytics without being overwhelmed by low-level PCAP details.
  • SOC / OT Analysts: Can drill down from any KPI into the Attack Path Investigation Workspace, inspect raw industrial protocols (S7comm, Modbus, OPC UA), view CVE CVSS scores, and trigger containment actions.
  • Plant Operators: "Why It Matters" callouts translate cyber vulnerabilities into tangible physical outcomes (e.g., "Turbogenerator Unit 4 emergency mechanical overspeed trip").

3. Preventing Attack-Path Visual Overload

Traditional network graph tools render unreadable "hairballs" of thousands of interconnected nodes. We prevent cognitive overload by:

  • Purdue Model Visual Swimlanes: Organizing nodes strictly across Levels 0 through 4 to reflect industrial hierarchy.
  • Path-Focused Traversal: Isolating the highest-risk attack trajectories rather than dumping the whole topology.
  • Progressive Disclosure: Keeping detailed packet flows in the right-hand Investigation Drawer, opening only on demand.
  • Dimming & Highlighting: Selecting a node or edge visibly dims non-essential graph elements.

4. Handling Uncertainty & Incomplete Telemetry

Critical Principle: "Never represent incomplete data as a confirmed safe state." When sensors degrade or packet loss occurs (demonstrated in the "Degraded Telemetry" demo state), the system refuses to show zero alerts. Instead, a prominent warning banner appears, confidence scores drop below 70%, and affected nodes display dashed magenta borders indicating unconfirmed data.

5. Scaling to Enterprise OT Environments

In large installations with tens of thousands of I/O points, the architecture scales through:

  • Hierarchical aggregation: Grouping L0/L1 devices under parent RTUs and PLCs.
  • Blast radius filtering: Computing upstream entry points and downstream actuators dynamically.
  • Confidence thresholds: Allowing analysts to filter out low-confidence speculative paths.

6. Assumptions & Architectural Boundaries

We assumed standard ISA/IEC 62443 Purdue architecture with dual-homed DMZ jump hosts and edge firewalls. Fictional asset names (Siemens S7-1500, OSIsoft PI, Palo Alto) were selected to reflect standard manufacturing and power generation topologies. All code is 100% native HTML5, CSS3, and ES6+ with zero third-party dependencies.
